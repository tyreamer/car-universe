# Research standards

1. Establish market, model years, chassis, body, engine and gearbox. Record only the selected configuration. Explain material differences where adjacent entries share displacement or gearbox.
2. Match eligible U.S. EPA rows by year, model label and powertrain. Preserve the selected row IDs. Consult an applicable period or manufacturer source for trim and chassis, and document disagreements.
3. Record output, weight and equipment with their stated year and measurement basis. Period test weights are specific to a tested vehicle. Leave unresolved fields null.
4. Separate routine upkeep, major repair exposure, prevention and observed failure incidence. A bulletin shows a documented issue and an applicability range. A single repair bill describes one vehicle.
5. Use firsthand tests for driving. Use published design references for descriptive tags and editorial styling opinions.
6. Apply rubric anchors and state the limits of each assessment. Use low confidence for provisional inferences. Leave the score null when the evidence cannot support an anchor.
7. Run validation and calibrate closely related configurations. Track every remaining research item and review the generated report.

The initial catalog includes a mixture of archived identities and EPA-supported model/powertrain matches awaiting trim-level corroboration. A research state records the kind of evidence present. It does not signify independent human approval. The date on an assessment records this editorial review.
