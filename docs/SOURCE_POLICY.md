# Source policy

Use manufacturer literature, original road tests, government datasets and relevant technical bulletins. Specialist documents can explain a mechanism or service procedure. Record commercial interests, single-car coverage and market differences. Each vehicle links material factual claims to source IDs.

The retained EPA row subset comes from the public [Fuel Economy Web Services download](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/ws/). EPA data describes tested or represented powertrain configurations. Emissions labels may group trims, omit old models or contain mismatches. Use additional archival sources to confirm specific trim, year or body claims and document disputes.

For example, the MY2000 MR2 Spyder EPA row lists front-wheel drive. The period U.S. road test establishes the mid-engine, rear-drive configuration. The facelift U.S. manual remained five-speed. Toyota GB describes a six-speed three-pedal model for its market.

Use paraphrased findings and facts. Do not republish proprietary datasets, full road tests, service procedures or unlicensed images. The AWS and third-party archives in the registry identify the originating publisher when known. Preserve unknown authors or dates as null.

For photography, store a local optimized image and attribute the creator, source and license with any modifications.
